Containment is an easy metric. If the conversation never reaches a human, the dashboard looks efficient. Resolution is harder—and more honest.

A contained interaction can still leave the customer without an answer, without a completed transaction, or with a silent failure in a downstream system. In agentic customer service, that gap becomes expensive quickly: reopened tickets, repeat contacts, compliance exposure, and eroded trust.

## What containment hides

Common failure modes that still “contain”:

- The agent gives a confident but incorrect answer
- A tool call fails and the agent apologizes without completing the work
- The customer abandons after friction that never triggers transfer
- Policy-sensitive cases are closed without proper disclosure or escalation

Those sessions may improve containment and hurt business outcomes at the same time.

## Measure completed work

Pair containment with resolution metrics such as:

- Correct task completion verified against systems of record
- Containment with resolution (not containment alone)
- Repeat contact within a defined window
- Transfer success with context preservation when humans are needed
- Cost per successful outcome

## Use the same taxonomy in pilot and production

If evaluation during vendor selection measures resolution and tool success, production monitoring should reuse that taxonomy. Otherwise teams optimize for the metric that is easiest to report—not the one that protects the customer experience.

When containment is the headline KPI, ask what completed work looks like for each journey. That question usually reveals whether the AI program is ready for scale.
