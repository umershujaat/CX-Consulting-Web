Enterprise AI contact-center decisions move quickly. Vendor demos look polished. Feature matrices look complete. Yet many RFPs still ask the wrong questions—and then struggle when the pilot cannot survive production traffic.

A practical RFP is not a longer questionnaire. It is a buyer-side instrument that forces vendors to prove workflow depth, integration reality, evaluation evidence, and commercial clarity under conditions that resemble your environment.

## Start with outcomes, not features

Before listing capabilities, define the customer journeys and business outcomes the platform must support:

- Which intents should be fully resolved without a human?
- Where should AI assist an agent instead of replacing one?
- What does “successful resolution” mean for each journey?
- Which systems of record must be updated for the work to count as complete?

If those answers are fuzzy, every vendor will look strong—and none will be comparable.

## Require scenario-based proof

Ask vendors to run your scenarios, not theirs. Include:

1. Happy-path resolution with tool calls into CRM or ticketing
2. Ambiguous customer language and mid-call corrections
3. Interruptions, barge-in, and silence handling for voice
4. Authentication, payment, or policy-sensitive edge cases
5. Escalation with full context preservation to a human agent
6. Downstream failure: timeout, partial update, or idempotent retry

Score the outcome, not the narration. Capture transcripts, tool traces, latency, and failure recovery.

## Separate architecture claims from operating claims

A platform can be architecturally sound and still be operationally immature. Require written answers on:

- Observability: what quality, latency, and tool-failure signals are available on day one?
- Change control: how are prompts, tools, and workflows versioned and regression-tested?
- Data handling: retention, residency, redaction, and access controls for PII/PCI
- Human oversight: approval gates, disclosure, and auditability for agentic actions

## Normalize commercial models early

Do not wait for the shortlist to discover that vendors price on incompatible units. Ask for a three-year model under your volume assumptions, including:

- Per-minute, per-conversation, per-resolution, and token or tool fees
- Minimum commits, overages, professional services, and support tiers
- Cost per successful outcome, not only license price

## Make the decision auditable

A defensible selection leaves a trail: weighted requirements, scored scenarios, reference checks, residual risks, and a clear recommendation. That record is what procurement, security, and executive sponsors need when the program moves from pilot to production.
